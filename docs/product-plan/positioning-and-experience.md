# Kiara: positioning, activation, and customer experience

Product design proposal · 26 September 2026 · Forward-looking throughout. Customer choices, prices, targets, and examples below are hypotheses or product decisions, not claims about an operating product or observed results. Competitor descriptions reflect primary product pages reviewed on this date.

## 1. The product customers should buy

**Keep legal in step with your business.**

Kiara watches the company systems its customer chooses, understands what changed, and prepares the legal work that change may require. It brings the business owner and counsel a sourced explanation, proposed document changes, and the decisions needed to finish the work. It follows the resulting matter through approval and execution, then records what the company decided so the next matter starts with better context.

The unit of value is a **resolved matter**: a business change with a documented outcome. **Verified completion** means every required approved action is verified; **closed—no action** means an authorized reviewer recorded why no action is needed. A specialist handoff remains open until the required work is resolved. Track handoffs separately from completed work. Alerts, drafts and chat answers are intermediate outputs.

The strongest reason to buy is practical: “Our lawyer should receive the relevant facts and a useful first draft without my team spending hours reconstructing what happened.” Founders get fewer surprise escalations. Product teams know which facts legal needs before launch. Counsel spends more time on decisions and less on assembling background. None of these benefits requires a promise that software replaces legal judgment.

## 2. Choose a customer whose everyday work fits the product

Design around **20–200-person B2B software companies**, initially with a US legal entity, customers in declared markets, GitHub plus Slack and Google Drive, and a founder, operations leader, or small legal team responsible for coordinating outside or fractional counsel. This is a focused design assumption, not a claim that all companies in this range have the same needs. Supported jurisdictions must come from configured legal playbooks and counsel expertise, not company headquarters alone.

The ideal company ships frequently, adds vendors and AI features, answers enterprise diligence questions, and has a recognizable set of privacy notices, terms, data-processing agreements, customer agreements, vendor contracts, and internal policies. Those documents already exist somewhere but lose alignment as the company changes.

| Person | What they need from Kiara | Main product surface |
|---|---|---|
| Founder / COO, usually buyer | Know what needs a decision, who owns it, and whether work is finished | Inbox, matter status, measured value |
| Operations / legal coordinator, daily owner | Gather facts once; route work; chase the right person | Inbox, Company context, Connections |
| Product / engineering owner | Answer a precise factual question in context | Slack prompt, PR context, evidence confirmation |
| Fractional / outside counsel | Review complete facts, assumptions, precedents, and a controlled redline | Restricted matter room and Word export |
| Security / privacy owner | Confirm actual data flows and customer commitments | Impact review, data/vendor context |

Companies with no documents can onboard and establish a sourced baseline, but any drafted starting documents remain subject to the configured legal review. Specialist domains—regulated healthcare, regulated financial services, disputes, employment actions, tax, and corporate transactions—need domain-specific workflows and qualified reviewers. They should not silently inherit the standard software-company workflow.

## 4. The recurring jobs that earn a place in the company

Every job shares one matter model and one approval history. The examples below describe product behavior, not conclusions that a specific legal obligation necessarily applies.

| Business change | Kiara prepares | Evidence required before a conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| New vendor receives customer data | Vendor/data-flow comparison, relevant agreement clauses, candidate subprocessor-list or notice edits, counsel questions | Actual purpose, data categories, destinations, vendor terms, applicable company/customer commitments |
| Feature changes collection, retention, sharing or AI use | Impact brief and proposed changes to affected notices, terms, policies or assessments | Shipping status, actual configuration, user population, current approved documents |
| Sales commits to a new service level or security practice | Comparison with the approved position, implementation owner, proposed contractual response | Exact promise, counterparty, relevant agreement, business feasibility confirmation |
| Customer agreement creates a time-bound obligation | Named owner, due date, evidence requirement and completion task | Executed text, effective date, exceptions and amendments |
| An approved business practice changes | Cross-document discrepancy report and controlled update bundle | Approved new facts, affected versions, scope and decision authority |

Support manual intake, uploads, links, email forwarding and a signed event API alongside named connectors. An unfamiliar event enters the same classification and evidence workflow; it does not imply the product understands every source or legal domain automatically.

The canonical demonstration is fictional **Northstar**, which adds **RelayAI** to summarize support transcripts. A PR suggests the integration; the connected product discussion supplies intended use; an owner confirms whether production data will be sent. Kiara links the approved vendor record, privacy notice and relevant contractual commitments. It presents potential changes with source citations and unresolved questions. Counsel can reject the need for an edit or revise the proposal. Once approved, the designated publisher applies the approved versions and Kiara verifies the recorded result. The PR alone never proves the feature is live or that customer notice is legally required.

## 5. The experience starts with a useful baseline

The activation target is **one working session to a sourced baseline and one reviewable matter**, conditional on account permissions, source availability and reviewer participation. This is a design target, not a guarantee of legal readiness. A clearly marked rehearsal using fictional data is available immediately; it never counts as a real customer outcome.

1. **Define the workspace.** Ask for legal entity, products, declared markets, business owner, counsel contact and first workflow. Offer “I don't know” for facts needing confirmation.
2. **Choose what Kiara can see.** Pick named repositories, Slack channels and Drive folders. Show requested permissions, excluded areas, and who will be able to see resulting matters. Reading sources and publishing changes are separate permissions.
3. **Identify authoritative documents.** Suggest candidate documents; have the owner confirm current versions, signed agreements, templates and retired copies. Manual upload is a supported path when connection approval is pending.
4. **Confirm the company map.** Present extracted products, vendors, data categories, business commitments and owners. Every fact has a source and date. Conflicts and unknowns appear in a short queue; inferred facts are not labeled confirmed.
5. **Set decision rules.** Choose who confirms business facts, approves legal language, authorizes sending to counsel, and publishes. Seed these choices from an editable workflow template.
6. **Review one matter.** Show a genuine candidate found in the permitted lookback period or explicitly enter rehearsal mode. The user confirms the factual interpretation before reviewing proposed legal impact.

At completion, the workspace shows **Connected coverage**, **Confirmed context**, **Missing context**, and **First matter**. “Connected coverage” is concrete: which sources are included, last successful sync, known gaps and permission limitations. A disconnect or stale feed changes the visible coverage status. The interface must never imply “all clear” from an empty queue when relevant sources are missing.

Activation is recorded when an authorized user confirms a sourced business fact, completes the first review decision, and assigns the next step. Connecting three tools without using the output is setup, not activation.

## 6. The product should feel calm and accountable

The web application navigation is **Inbox · Documents · Company context · Playbooks & learning · Connections · Activity**. Inbox contains the matter list and detail views. It answers: What needs my decision? What is waiting on somebody else? What was completed? What can Kiara currently see?

An individual matter contains the business change in plain English, cited evidence, affected documents and commitments, open factual questions, proposed changes, assigned reviewers, due dates and the decision record. Display factual confidence separately from urgency and proposed legal significance. A low-confidence signal may still deserve quick factual confirmation.

Notifications ask for one next action. A Slack message might say: “RelayAI may receive support transcripts. Maya, confirm which data is sent before Thursday's release. Review the evidence in Kiara.” Sensitive clauses and full document contents stay behind the matter's access controls. The notification is an invitation to a scoped decision, not an implicit approval. Reminders follow the customer's escalation policy and quiet hours; urgent routing is explicitly configured.

Counsel receives a bounded work packet: objective, verified facts, uncertainties, relevant source excerpts, current documents, proposed redlines, company precedent, and exact questions. Guest access is restricted to the matter. Business owners can confirm facts without gaining access to privileged or otherwise restricted material. The product should not label all lawyer-associated content privileged automatically.

Every correction can become a proposed context update or reusable rule. Show the user the scope: this matter, this document type, this counterparty, or company-wide. Approved learning should make subsequent work more consistent, while retaining its author, evidence and effective dates. A single accepted draft must not silently become policy.

## 7. Website copy and presentation

The website should explain an observable workflow before discussing the harness. Navigation: **Product · How it works · Integrations · Security · Pricing**. Primary CTA: **Start your workspace**. Secondary CTA: **Explore a sample matter**. The sample requires no account and uses labeled fictional data.

**Hero**

> Keep legal in step with your business.
>
> Kiara connects the changes happening across your company to the legal work they may require. Get the context, proposed document updates, and the right approvals in one place.
>
> Start your workspace · Explore a sample matter

The hero render shows the Northstar matter, three linked sources, “Business facts confirmed,” “Counsel review needed,” and a proposed document change. Use a visible “Sample workspace” label. Avoid fabricated customer logos, savings percentages or invented confidence scores.

**Section 2: From a business change to a completed decision.** A five-step visual: “Connect your sources → Understand the change → Prepare the work → Get the right approval → Verify completion.” Each step opens the corresponding moment in the sample matter.

**Section 3: The changes worth catching.** Four examples: “A new vendor touches customer data”; “A product changes how data is used”; “A customer asks for a new commitment”; “An agreement creates a deadline.” Each describes the evidence collected and the reviewable output.

**Section 4: Bring your lawyer the whole picture.** Copy: “Share the business facts, relevant agreements, proposed redlines, and open questions in one controlled matter. Your counsel makes the legal decisions with the context already assembled.” Render the restricted counsel review view.

**Section 5: Your company context, with receipts.** Copy: “Know where each fact came from, when it was confirmed, and which decisions shaped the recommendation. Correct the context once; review how that correction applies next time.” Show a fact card and scoped learning approval.

**Section 6: Start with the tools you use.** Display supported connectors and exact coverage, then manual intake and API options. Do not display unavailable integrations as installed features. Link each connector to permissions, supported event types, synchronization behavior and setup requirements.

**Section 7: Control what is read, shared and changed.** Explain source selection, matter access, approval rules, audit history, deletion and export. Security certifications and residency options appear only once independently substantiated and actually available.

**Closing CTA:** “Start with one workflow. Build a clearer picture of your company.” FAQ answers cover lawyer involvement, incomplete context, approvals, source access, correction handling and how measured value is calculated. Avoid guarantees that Kiara catches everything or never repeats a mistake.

## 8. Grow through completed work and trusted collaboration

The useful growth loop is internal: a matter asks a product owner for a fact, the owner sees why it matters, and the team gains a reusable approved answer. Inviting counsel improves the matter packet and makes future collaboration easier. Invite actions disclose what will be shared and require an authorized user; no automatic contact harvesting or unsolicited outreach.

A fractional counsel practice can manage separate client workspaces and recommend a reviewed workflow template. Templates must contain generic rules or explicitly approved reusable content; private client documents, embeddings and negotiated positions never move between clients by default. Aggregate benchmarking is optional and should be built only after a defensible privacy design and sufficient cohort size exist.

Retention should come from accurate context, fewer repeated questions and completed work—not notification volume. A weekly digest can show decisions needed, completed matters, stale sources and approved context changes. It should remain quiet when there is nothing actionable, according to workspace preferences.

## 9. Measure savings before advertising them

Use the customer as the unit of comparison and matter type as the comparison stratum. Establish a short baseline using recent comparable matters, customer time estimates and invoices where voluntarily provided. Separate measured activity from self-reported estimates.

Track business preparation minutes, counsel review time where available, clarification rounds, total elapsed time, reopened matters, incorrect proposed impacts, missed known incidents, and completed matters with sufficient evidence. Also measure the review burden created by false positives. Otherwise, apparent automation can simply move work onto reviewers.

**Estimated net time saved = comparable baseline human time − all human time with Kiara, including allocated setup and correction effort.** Count each activity once across all participating roles. **Estimated fee change = comparable historical billed fees − current comparable billed fees**, adjusted for scope and pricing differences. If no invoice evidence exists, label the result “estimated counsel hours affected,” not “dollars saved.” Fixed-fee counsel arrangements do not translate hours saved directly into lower invoices.

The central product score is the rate of eligible business changes brought to a documented, verified decision before the customer-defined deadline. Pair it with evidence quality, false-positive burden, reopened decisions and audited missed events. No single percentage should hide gaps in coverage.

## 10. Packaging and decisions to validate

Package around one company workspace and monitored scope, with included human collaborators and counsel guests. Charging for every fact-checking participant would discourage the collaboration the product needs. Usage limits should be understandable—connected scope, historical ingestion allowance and declared matter-processing capacity—with visible controls before overages. Do not meter by alerts or create incentives to generate more matters.

| Package hypothesis | Intended need | Included product |
|---|---|---|
| Team | One company coordinating business owners and counsel | Core connectors, context, matters, redlines, approvals, counsel guests, audit/export |
| Scale | Multiple entities or sophisticated governance | Entity boundaries, SSO/SCIM where available, retention controls, advanced routing, API and integration support |
| Counsel workspace | Fractional or outside counsel serving multiple clients | Strictly separated client workspaces, scoped guest work, approved template distribution |

All packages need baseline security and approval controls. Counsel's professional fees remain separate and explicit. Numeric prices should follow paid design-partner willingness-to-pay tests and actual processing costs; there is no validated price in this proposal. A guided evaluation should demonstrate a real workflow with a capped allowance and exportable results, without silently granting publishing authority.

| Decision | Chosen direction | Evidence that would change it |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Software teams with frequent changes and an identifiable legal owner | Repeated lack of actionable changes or inability to assign owners |
| Promise | Keep legal documents and decisions aligned with business changes | Users consistently value a different recurring outcome more |
| Primary object | Matter with evidence, decisions and verified completion | Existing customer systems can deliver the same closure with less effort |
| Activation | Sourced baseline plus first real decision in one session where access permits | Permissions or context work repeatedly prevents useful early output |
| Differentiation | Proactive change detection plus complete business-to-counsel execution | Comparative customer trials show no meaningful workflow advantage |
| Learning | Reviewed, scoped context and decision reuse | Corrections fail to reduce repeat errors or increase review burden |
| Value claim | Customer-specific measured time and fee effects | Savings disappear after setup, false positives and correction work |

These choices describe one complete product. The validation work determines whether its promised workflow is valuable and reliable enough to earn continued use.
