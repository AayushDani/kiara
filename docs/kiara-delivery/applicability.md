# Agreement applicability review

Target: v2 §§7–9, 15 J05, 16. Implementation: `src/v2/inventory.ts`, `src/v2/applicability.ts`, Work → Evidence, local/model proposal adoption, approval freshness checks, and `tests/v2-applicability.test.ts`.

The supplied agreement register is an expiring named fact-owner attestation over every current agreement and amendment head in one matter audience. It is not a claim that every external customer agreement has been discovered. A legal reviewer assesses every row exactly once. A resolved `notice_required` or `no_notice` row cites an exact character span of an executed document and states a reason. A required notice additionally records a specific trigger, distinct recipients, delivery channel and timing; incomplete details cannot be saved as a resolved required notice. Draft records and unresolved questions stay `unknown`.

The assessment binds the precise matter objective, declared counterparty, declared products, jurisdiction, selected confirmed matter facts, every current matter fact and source, register hash, document hashes, reviewer membership version and source permissions. Decisive rows require a declared counterparty and at least one selected confirmed matter fact. Nested dependencies must share the matter audience. Changes to the target, factual basis, register, clause text, source access or reviewer authority make the assessment stale. A later assessment supersedes prior assessments for that matter permanently, even if the later one is withdrawn.

Prepared proposals copy the current matrix and its exact assessment hash, agreement revision/content hash, clause offsets, reviewer, reason and notice details. A newer review makes the prior proposal ineligible for approval until preparation is repeated. Older rows lacking required notice details are stale. The matrix is legal review of the named target and supplied register; it does not itself approve the proposal, authorize a notice, or establish missing legal coverage. Unknown rows remain visible as unresolved questions for owner review.

Synthetic tests cover malformed commands, reviewer roles, exact clauses, draft/unknown rows, target fact and population changes, later-review withdrawal, private-source dependency laundering, proposal staleness, local restart, normalized serialization, tenant isolation and source deletion. Actual legal correctness, complete customer inventory and provider delivery require separate qualified/customer evidence.
